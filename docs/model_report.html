⚓ Base Rate — Exponentiated Intercept (exp β₀) — Anchor of the Pricing Equation
$1,614
The Base Rate is exp(β₀) = exp(7.3863) = $1,614. It is the first factor every underwriter multiplies against — without it, the individual relativities below are meaningless. All continuous rating factors (age, BMI, etc.) then multiply the base rate using their raw covariate values.
E[Y] = exp(β₀) × exp(βage · age) × exp(βbmi · bmi) × exp(βchildren · children) × exp(βsmoker · smoker) × exp(βregion)
      = $1,614 × exp(0.02864 · age) × exp(0.01412 · bmi) × exp(0.08417 · children) × exp(1.5003 · smoker) × exp(βregion)
Example (age 40, BMI 28, non-smoker, NE): $1,614 × exp(0.02864×40) × exp(0.01412×28) = $1,614 × 3.162 × 1.480 ≈ $7,551 pure premium

⚠ Analyst Note — Decile 10 Tail Over-Prediction

Decile 10 shows an A/E ratio of 0.74: the model predicts an average of $48,931 but actual mean claims are only $36,399 — an over-prediction of approximately $12,500 per policy. This is a well-documented behaviour of Gamma GLMs with a log link and is not a model failure, but it must be understood and defended.

Why it happens: The log link is multiplicative. When multiple high-risk factors compound simultaneously (e.g. older age + high BMI + tobacco use), the exponentiation extrapolates very aggressively into the sparse extreme right tail, overshooting the actual observed costs where the training data is thin.

How a production team would address this:

  • Winsorise large claims before fitting — cap the top 1–5% of charges (e.g. at the 99th percentile, ~$50k) before running the GLM. This prevents the model from over-fitting to extreme observations while keeping the bulk of the distribution well-calibrated.
  • Two-component large-loss model — fit the main Gamma GLM for claims below a large-loss threshold (e.g. $25k), then fit a separate Pareto or lognormal model for the tail, blending them with a mixing weight. This is standard practice in reinsurance pricing.
  • Tweedie GLM — a compound Poisson-Gamma model that handles zero-inflated, heavy-tailed aggregate loss distributions more naturally. The Tweedie power parameter is estimated from the data and adapts the tail behaviour automatically.
  • A/E monitoring at next filing — in a live portfolio, this decile would be flagged for a rate revision at the annual rate review, with credibility-weighted rate changes applied to the top-decile segment.
